# Viva Presentation: Storyline Draft

**Thesis:** AI-Driven Transformation in Banking GRC: Implications for Workforce Management in UK and EU Banks
**Length:** 20 slides, about 18–20 minutes, plus a Q&A slide
**Palette:** white background; black text; grey for supporting text, shapes and baseline bars; **red only for the one point each slide must land**
**Rule for every slide:** the title is the takeaway sentence, there are no more than 3–4 short lines of text, and there is one visual

---

## The story in one paragraph

Banks are putting AI into risk and compliance faster than their people can govern it, and regulators now hold those people accountable. The literature explains the technology, the jobs and the acceptance of AI, but only separately. So I asked practitioners what is really happening, turned what they told me into testable hypotheses, and tested them on public industry data. The numbers back the practitioners: AI is surging, governance structures are being built, but reskilling lags and expert knowledge is at risk. That gap led to a seven-stage workforce framework for GRC and HR leaders.

**Story arc:** Why → What we know → How we studied it → Where the hypotheses came from → Why these tests → What we found → What it means → What banks should do

## ACT 3: HOW I STUDIED IT (METHODOLOGY)

### Slide 7: A sequential exploratory mixed-methods design
- **On slide:** three steps, left to right:
  1. **Listen:** interviews and questionnaire (themes T1–T10)
  2. **Test:** hypotheses H5–H10 on public data (D1–D4)
  3. **Compare:** triangulation, giving verdicts on P1–P4 and the framework
- **Visual:** three chevrons, with step 2 outlined in red
- **Say:** "The interviews decided *what* to test. The public data decided *whether it holds* across the industry. The public datasets are independent sources; they were not produced from my interviews."

### Slide 8: Two kinds of evidence
- **On slide:**
  - **Primary:** written semi-structured interviews (11 questions, 5 sections) and a questionnaire (25 Likert items, 7 constructs adapted from Davis, Glikson & Woolley, and Brougham & Haar). Purposive and snowball sampling.
  - **Secondary (public):**
    - **D1:** Eurostat AI adoption, 27 EU countries, 2021–2025
    - **D2:** annual reports of large UK and EU banks, FY2022 and FY2025, 19 paired banks
    - **D3:** 163 UK GRC job adverts
    - **D4:** Bank of England/FCA, IIA and Thomson Reuters surveys
- **Visual:** two columns, with four small grey icons for D1–D4
- **Say:** "Each public dataset answers a different question: the economy, the banks, the job market and the profession."

## ACT 4: WHERE THE HYPOTHESES CAME FROM, AND WHY THESE TESTS

### Slide 9: Every hypothesis traces back to something practitioners said
- **On slide:** a mapping table (theme → questionnaire signal → hypothesis):
  - T1 "AI is spreading, still pilot-heavy" → role will change 4.33 → **H5a, H8** adoption rising
  - T2/T3 "routine work absorbed" → usefulness 3.58 → **H10a, H6** AI disclosure and skill mix
  - T4/T5 "humans must challenge AI" → can defend decision 4.33 → **H10b** AI governance bodies
  - T6 "regulation felt unevenly" → oversight clear 3.67 → **H10a (regulation), H10d** UK vs EU
  - T7 "hybrid skills" → data skills 4.00 → **H5b, H6, H7**
  - T8/T9 "training lags" → training adequate **3.33** (in red) → **H9, H10c**
- **Visual:** a three-column flow with arrows, with the training row in red
- **Say:** "This is the bridge in my design. Each test exists because a practitioner theme pointed to it."

### Slide 10: Each test was chosen by the shape of the data
- **On slide:** a decision tree:
  - Same units measured twice → **paired t-test + Wilcoxon** (H5a, H10a); **Friedman** across 4 years
  - Yes/no for the same banks in two years → **exact McNemar** (H10b)
  - Two independent, skewed groups → **Mann–Whitney U** (H7, H10d)
  - Yes/no skill flags across groups → **chi-square / Fisher + Holm correction** (H6)
  - Only published percentages → **z-tests** (H8, H9)
  - Strength of a link → **Pearson / Spearman / OLS** (H5b, H10c)
- **Footer (in red):** the questionnaire is descriptive only (means and SD against the midpoint of 3), because significance tests are not meaningful on a small purposive sample
- **Visual:** a grey decision tree with test names in black
- **Say:** "Non-parametric tests sit alongside the t-tests because bank data is small and skewed. Holm correction stops multiple testing producing false positives."

## ACT 5: WHAT I FOUND (ANALYSIS AND INTERPRETATION)

### Slide 11: Practitioners: routine work goes, judgement and knowledge matter more
- **On slide:** three quote cards:
  - "GRC testing roles have disappeared."
  - "C-level execs really do not understand what the AI is, and thus are likely to simply trust it."
  - "Too much business knowledge exists in people's heads… prioritise knowledge capture before any significant workforce reductions."
  - Strip below: the 10 themes grouped under objectives (i)–(iv)
- **Visual:** quote cards with a red left border
- **Say:** "Three messages stood out: routine work is absorbed, the accountable people may understand AI least, and expert knowledge is at risk."

### Slide 12: Questionnaire: tools are easy to use but hard to see into, and training lags
- **On slide:** a horizontal bar chart of the 7 construct means (1–5 scale, midpoint line at 3); a callout pair showing ease of use 4.33 vs understanding 3.33, and training adequacy 3.33 in red
- **Visual:** grey bars, red for training, a dashed line at 3
- **Say:** "All constructs are above neutral, but the gaps between them tell the story: usable, not transparent; skills rising, training behind."

### Slide 13: AI adoption is surging everywhere (H5a, H8, H10a: supported)
- **On slide:**
  - Large EU firms using AI: **27.5% → 53.6%** (2021–2025), up in all 27 countries, p < 0.0001
  - UK financial firms using AI: **58% → 75%**, p = 0.008
  - AI references in bank reports: **about 10×**, up in **19 of 19** banks, p < 0.0001
- **Visual:** a line chart of EU adoption (grey line, red line for large firms) and a slope chart of 19 banks FY2022 → FY2025
- **Say:** "Three independent sources, one direction."

### Slide 14: Governance structures are growing faster than governance practice (H10a regulation, H10b, H10d)
- **On slide:**
  - Banks naming an AI governance body: **5% → 42%** (McNemar p = 0.016)
  - AI Act / DORA / SS1/23 references: near zero → routine (p < 0.0001)
  - But AI-governance wording is **flat** (p ≈ 0.07), shown in red
  - UK banks discuss AI and AI governance more than EU banks
- **Visual:** two bars (FY2022 grey, FY2025 red) for governance bodies, plus a small UK vs EU dot plot
- **Say:** "Banks are building committees and citing regulations. Practitioners say oversight is not yet embedded in daily work, and the data agrees."

### Slide 15: Skills are shifting, but reskilling is not keeping up (H6, H7, H9, H10c)
- **On slide:**
  - AI-exposed adverts vs others: data **51% vs 25%**, automation **49% vs 13%**, cyber **19% vs 6%**, regulatory **40% vs 78%** (all Holm p < 0.05)
  - AI-titled roles pay a median of £90k vs £60k (p = 0.009, small group)
  - Reskilling disclosure is **not linked** to AI intensity (ρ = −0.16), shown in red
- **Visual:** a paired bar chart of the four skills (grey = other, red = AI-exposed)
- **Say:** "Employers hire technical profiles for AI work, yet everyone says regulatory judgement still matters. That is the hybrid gap. Meanwhile, reskilling talk has not kept pace with AI talk."

### Slide 16: Hypothesis scorecard
- **On slide:** a table of H1–H10 with a verdict marker:
  - **Supported:** H1–H3 (descriptive), H5a, H5b, H6, H8, H10a (AI, reskilling, regulation), H10b
  - **Partly supported:** H4, H7, H9, H10d
  - **Not supported:** H10a (AI-governance wording), H10c
- **Visual:** black ● = supported, grey ◐ = partly, red ○ = not supported
- **Say:** "The unsupported results matter as much as the supported ones. They show where banks are lagging."

### Slide 17: Triangulation: all four propositions hold
- **On slide:**
  - P1: Supported (interviews, job descriptions, H5a/H8/H10a)
  - P2: Supported in texts and structures; partly in practice
  - P3: Supported (interviews, adverts, H6/H5b/H9)
  - P4: Supported qualitatively, an emergent contribution (in red)
- **Visual:** a 4-row table with columns for interviews | questionnaire | public data | verdict
- **Say:** "Where practitioners and public data agree, I'm confident. Where only practitioners speak, as on knowledge loss, I say so."

## ACT 6: WHAT IT MEANS, AND WHAT BANKS SHOULD DO

### Slide 18: AI changes the composition of GRC work, not just its volume
- **On slide:** the role-impact map in four quadrants:
  - **Automated:** data consolidation, reconciliation, first-level alert triage
  - **Augmented:** investigation, KYC judgement, model validation
  - **Newly created:** AI governance lead, AI model risk validator, human-in-the-loop supervisor
  - **Human-retained:** interpreting regulatory intent, challenge, final accountability (in red)
- **Visual:** a 2×2 grid, with the human-retained quadrant outlined in red
- **Say:** "Jobs are recomposed, not simply removed, which is exactly Autor's argument, now seen inside a bank."

### Slide 19: A seven-stage framework, in which knowledge protection comes before restructuring
- **On slide:** seven chevrons: Diagnose → **Protect knowledge** (in red) → Redesign roles → Build oversight competence → Reskill and source → Secure trust → Lead change; below them, the enablers: data quality, accountability map, workforce indicators
- **Visual:** a chevron flow with stage 2 in red
- **Say:** "The most important design choice is the order: capture what experts know *before* cutting roles, because those experts are the ones who validate the AI."

### Slide 20: Recommendations, conclusion and limits
- **On slide:**
  - **GRC leaders:** map activities, not job titles; capture knowledge first
  - **HR:** close the training gap with role-tiered AI literacy; create career paths for junior staff
  - **Boards and senior managers:** close the AI-understanding gap at the top; pair every AI plan with a people plan
  - **Limits:** small purposive primary sample; disclosure counts are words, not actions; 19 paired banks; one UK job board
  - **Next:** larger surveys, longitudinal tracking, EU job data
- **Visual:** three columns for the audiences, with a thin grey band at the bottom for limits and future work
- **Say:** "AI in GRC succeeds only if the people who answer for it can understand and challenge it."
