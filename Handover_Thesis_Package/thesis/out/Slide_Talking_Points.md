# Slide-by-Slide Talking Points (5–6 min)
*Look at the slide, hit the pointers, speak in your own words. Bold = the one thing to land.*

---

## Slide 1: Title (≈20 s)
- Greet the panel; give your name and that this is the Second Review.
- One-line topic: *how AI is changing GRC work in UK and EU banks, and what that means for managing people.*
- **Hook:** "Banks are adopting AI in compliance faster than they're building the people who must oversee it."

## Slide 2: Why this study matters (≈40 s)
- **Adoption is mainstream:** 75% of UK financial firms use AI, and another 10% plan to.
- **Dependence:** risk and compliance relies heavily on third-party AI (64%), so banks use tools they didn't build.
- **Understanding gap:** 46% understand their AI only partially.
- **Talent demand:** AI risk and compliance job postings are up 187%.
- **Regulation:** DORA, the AI Act, SS1/23 and SM&CR make competent human oversight a legal expectation.
- **Land it:** "Adoption is outpacing the workforce capability needed to govern it. That's the gap I study."

## Slide 3: Objectives (≈30 s)
- Primary objective in one breath: role structure, skills, staffing and adaptation, leading to a framework.
- Point to the five secondary objectives quickly: map tasks, regulation, skills, employee attitudes, framework.
- **Land it:** "The objectives are unchanged from Review 1. I've only extended the regulatory scope to cover the 2026 AI Act changes, Consumer Duty, the EU AML rules and the ECB's priorities."
- Don't read every objective aloud.

## Slide 4: Methodology (≈45 s)
- Three evidence streams, left to right:
  1. **Written interviews** with senior practitioners, analysed thematically for depth.
  2. **Questionnaire:** 25 Likert items in 7 constructs based on TAM, STARA and AI trust, used to check the themes.
  3. **Secondary data:** regulations, supervisory surveys and job postings, content-analysed for breadth.
- Triangulation produces propositions P1–P4 (tested qualitatively) and hypotheses H1–H4 (tested on the questionnaire).
- Ethics: purposive and snowball sampling, informed consent, fully anonymised, no bank named.
- **Land it:** "The aim is analytical, not statistical, generalisation. The value is depth from senior voices, cross-checked against large public datasets."

## Slide 5: Key findings from practitioners (≈50 s)
- **Maturity:** rules-based automation is mature, but AI is mostly still at pilot stage.
- **What goes:** routine reporting, reconciliation and monitoring.
- **What stays:** judgement, such as challenging risk decisions, interpreting regulation and agreeing remediation.
- **Roles:** testing roles are shrinking, while review and design roles are growing.
- **Human review is non-negotiable:** because of errors and hallucination risk.
- **Hidden risk:** the experts whose routine work AI absorbs are the same people who know enough to check the AI.
- Read **one** quote, the C-level one, then pause: *"C-level execs really do not understand what the AI is, and thus are likely to simply trust it."*
- **Land it:** "Knowledge capture has to come before workforce reduction."

## Slide 6: Questionnaire results (≈40 s)
- Every construct sits above the midpoint of 3, so attitudes are broadly positive.
- **Usability–transparency gap:** the tools are easy to use (4.33) but hard to see into (3.33). That's a recipe for automation bias.
- **Change expected:** people expect their role to change (4.33), but personal job worry varies most between respondents.
- **Training lags:** training adequacy is 3.33, while the need for data and judgement skills is 4.00.
- **Oversight on paper versus practice:** oversight exists (4.00) but isn't clearly applied in practice (3.67).
- **Land it:** "People are ready, but the organisation isn't yet equipping them."
- Mention that the scale is 1–5 and a higher replaceability score means more concern.

## Slide 7: How GRC jobs are changing (≈40 s)
- Use the table's pattern, not every row: **from doing the work to supervising the system that does it.**
- Pick two examples:
  - KYC/AML: from clearing alerts to investigating cases the AI prioritised.
  - Internal audit: the advisory share of the work rises from 24% to 41% by 2035.
- **New roles:** AI Compliance Officer (22% of postings), Model Risk Manager, Responsible AI Lead and others.
- **Hybrid skills earn a premium:** 24% for combining regulatory and model-lifecycle skills.
- Caveat in a few words: "The jobs data is mainly US, so I use it as a directional indicator."

## Slide 8: Workforce transformation framework (≈45 s)
- Sweep across the seven stages: diagnose, protect knowledge, redesign roles, build oversight competence, reskill and source, secure trust, lead change.
- **Stop on stage 2:** "This is the new element from my primary data. Capture knowledge before you restructure."
- Stage 4 links competence to DORA, the AI Act, SS1/23 and SM&CR, so capability becomes auditable.
- Stage 5 is build/buy/borrow: build hybrid skills, buy scarce specialists, borrow vendors under DORA's third-party controls.
- Enablers along the bottom: data quality, clear accountability and continuous monitoring.
- **Land it:** "Sequencing is the managerial insight."

## Slide 9: Conclusions & next steps (≈30 s)
- Read the four conclusions as one story: routine work is automated (P1), regulation demands competence (P2), hybrid skills are essential (P3), and knowledge loss is the hidden risk (P4).
- Next steps: widen the sample (compliance, financial crime, HR), run reliability and Wilcoxon tests, analyse anonymised bank disclosures, and validate the framework. Final report by 26 Oct 2026.
- **Close:** "AI doesn't remove the need for GRC people. It changes what they must know. Thank you, I'm happy to take questions."
