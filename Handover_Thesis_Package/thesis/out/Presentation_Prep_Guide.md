## Part C: Likely questions and model answers

### 1. Sample and methodology (expect these first)

**Q1. How many people did you interview / how many questionnaire responses?**
> "The sample is purposive and senior: practitioners in GRC technology, architecture, internal audit, risk and consulting across the UK and EU. I collected until the core themes stopped producing new insight. The questionnaire validates the interview themes rather than generalising statistically, and data collection is continuing to broaden the sample into first-line compliance, financial crime and HR."
*(If pressed for the exact number, give it honestly. Don't evade a direct question from the panel; then pivot to depth, seniority and triangulation.)*

**Q2. Your sample is small. Can you generalise?**
> "No, and I don't claim to. The study claims analytical generalisation: it explains mechanisms and patterns. Statistical generalisation isn't the aim. That's why I triangulate with regulatory texts, the BoE/FCA survey and job-posting data, which cover far larger populations."

**Q3. Why written interviews instead of live interviews?**
> "The respondents are senior and time-constrained. Written responses let them give considered, precise answers and remove transcription error. The trade-off is less probing, which I'm addressing in follow-up conversations."

**Q4. Why purposive sampling?**
> "A random sample of bank employees would mostly be people with no exposure to AI-enabled GRC. I needed respondents who could actually answer the questions."

**Q5. Some respondents have under two years' GRC experience, but your criterion was two years.**
> "That field captured GRC-specific experience. Those respondents bring long overall professional experience and current exposure to AI-enabled GRC processes, which is what the criterion was designed to ensure. I'll state this clearly as a note in the final report."

**Q6. Respondents are mostly from service providers or capability centres, not banks. Is that a bias?**
> "It's a limitation I acknowledge. But service providers and GCCs are where much of the AI-enabled GRC work is now delivered, so they have a direct view of the change. I'm adding in-bank first-line and HR respondents to balance it."

**Q7. What hypotheses did you test? The format asks for statistical tests.**
> "The design is qualitatively dominant, so I use four propositions tested by triangulation, plus four directional hypotheses on the questionnaire constructs against the neutral midpoint. Descriptively, H1–H3 are supported. H4, that trust is conditional, isn't supported on the means, but reliance shows the widest spread and the interviews strongly support it. Formal Wilcoxon signed-rank tests and Cronbach's alpha will run on the final data file."

**Q8. Why Wilcoxon and not a t-test?**
> "Likert data is ordinal and the sample is small and non-normal, so a non-parametric test is appropriate."

**Q9. Why haven't you calculated Cronbach's alpha yet?**
> "Alpha is unstable on a small dataset and would be misleading at this stage. I'll report it on the complete data file, as planned in Review 1."

**Q10. One respondent gave '5' to everything, including contradictory items.**
> "Yes, I flagged that as uniform responding. Items 30 and 31 point in opposite directions, so the pattern is detectable. I've kept it but interpret it cautiously, and I'll screen with the reverse-worded items in the final analysis."

### 2. Findings and contribution

**Q11. What is new in your study? Isn't 'AI automates routine work' obvious?**
> "The routine-versus-judgement split confirms Autor and Frey & Osborne. The new contribution is two-fold. First, in GRC the people whose routine work is most automatable often hold the tacit knowledge needed to *validate* the automation, so restructuring sequence becomes a control issue. Second, regulation turns workforce competence into a compliance obligation, which the general automation literature doesn't consider."

**Q12. What's the single most important finding?**
> "Knowledge capture must come before workforce reduction. Otherwise banks lose the capability to challenge AI output, while regulation holds named people accountable for it."

**Q13. You say AI is mostly at pilot stage, but 75% of firms use AI. Isn't that a contradiction?**
> "No. The 75% covers all AI use across firms, including chatbots, fraud and internal processes. Inside GRC specifically, practitioners describe mature rules-based automation but AI largely in pilots. The survey also shows compliance and reporting as a major *future* growth area, consistent with that."

**Q14. What does 'usability–transparency gap' mean?**
> "Staff find tools easy to operate (4.33) but hard to see into (3.33). That's dangerous in a regulated function: easy-to-use but opaque tools invite automation bias, exactly what the EU AI Act's human oversight article warns against."

**Q15. Are jobs being lost?**
> "Some are. Practitioners report testing roles disappearing. But the stronger pattern is recomposition: processing shrinks while review, investigation, model validation and AI governance roles grow. The shape moves from a pyramid to a diamond. The risk is the talent pipeline: if entry roles shrink, where do future experts come from?"

**Q16. Your job-market figures are from US postings. Are they valid for UK/EU?**
> "I use them as a directional indicator only and say so on the slide note. The role families and skill requirements match UK and EU regulatory drivers such as the AI Act, SS1/23 and DORA, and the UK BoE/FCA survey independently confirms the skills constraint."

### 3. Regulation (likely from a finance or risk examiner)

**Q17. How does SM&CR relate to AI?**
> "There's no dedicated AI senior manager. Regulators rely on existing SMFs and the duty to take reasonable steps. So an executive accountable for a process is accountable for AI used in it, which makes the finding that senior leaders may not understand AI a personal regulatory exposure."

**Q18. What does the EU AI Act require of staff?**
> "Article 4 requires AI literacy measures, recast in 2026 by the Digital Omnibus as a duty of means calibrated to role. Article 14 requires effective human oversight of high-risk systems: overseers who understand limits, resist automation bias and can override. High-risk obligations such as credit scoring were deferred, which gives banks a window to build capability."

**Q19. What does DORA have to do with workforce management?**
> "The management body must keep its ICT-risk knowledge current through training. There must be ICT security and resilience training for all staff, and auditors must have sufficient ICT expertise. Capability becomes auditable evidence, not just an HR objective."

**Q20. Why SS1/23 if it's about models?**
> "It applies to AI and ML models. It requires documented roles with the skills and experience needed, validators with technical expertise, business knowledge and the standing to escalate, and an accountable SMF. It's effectively a workforce specification for model risk."

**Q21. UK and EU are different regimes. Why treat them together?**
> "Banks operating across both face overlapping obligations, and the workforce implications converge: competence, oversight and accountability. I note national differences as a limitation."

### 4. Framework and recommendations

**Q22. How is your framework different from a generic change model like Kotter's?**
> "It's GRC-specific and regulation-linked. Stage 4 maps competence to DORA, the AI Act, SS1/23 and SM&CR, and stage 2, knowledge protection before restructuring, comes directly from my primary evidence. Generic change models don't address oversight obligations or tacit-knowledge risk."

**Q23. How would a bank actually use it?**
> "As a sequenced checklist for the GRC and HR leadership. Map activity exposure, identify knowledge-critical people, capture their knowledge, then rewrite job descriptions, build tiered AI literacy, decide build/buy/borrow for skills, and monitor trust and retention."

**Q24. How will you validate the framework?**
> "By reviewing it with selected senior respondents before the final report, which is listed in the work to be done."

**Q25. Build, buy or borrow: which do you recommend?**
> "A blend. Build the hybrid profile internally, because domain knowledge can't be bought quickly. Buy scarce AI-risk specialists. Borrow vendor capacity, but under DORA third-party controls, because accountability stays with the bank."

### 5. Curveballs

**Q26. Isn't this just your own employer's view?**
> "No. Respondents come from different organisations and roles, all anonymised, and the findings are triangulated against public regulatory and market evidence. No employer or client data is used."

**Q27. Why not name the banks?**
> "To protect respondent anonymity and avoid attributing practices to institutions without disclosed evidence. Bank disclosures will be analysed in anonymised form."

**Q28. What would change your conclusions?**
> "Evidence that AI in GRC moves quickly from pilot to full production with fully explainable outputs. That would reduce the oversight burden. Current supervisory evidence points the other way."

**Q29. What is the biggest limitation?**
> "Sample breadth. The perspectives lean towards technology and service providers, and the data is cross-sectional during an ongoing transition. I'm addressing the first by widening the sample; the second is inherent and stated."

**Q30. If you were a CHRO tomorrow, what's the first thing you'd do?**
> "Identify the knowledge-critical people in GRC and start structured knowledge capture before any AI-led restructuring decision is made."

## Part D: Weak spots and how to defend them

| Weak spot | Defence in one line |
|---|---|
| Small sample | Analytical generalisation, senior respondents, triangulated with large-population secondary data |
| Experience criterion | GRC-specific field; overall experience and AI exposure meet the purpose of the criterion |
| H4 not supported on means | Honest reporting; dispersion and interviews support it; formal test pending |
| Uniform responder | Flagged, detectable via opposing items, interpreted cautiously |
| US job data | Directional only, consistent with UK/EU regulatory drivers |
| Bank disclosures pending | Listed in work to be done; will be anonymised |
| Service-provider-heavy sample | That's where much GRC delivery now sits; widening to in-bank roles |
