# ABSTRACT

Banks in the United Kingdom (UK) and the European Union (EU) are adopting Artificial Intelligence (AI) across their Governance, Risk and Compliance (GRC) functions, in regulatory monitoring, customer and transaction screening, control testing and compliance reporting. The return on this investment depends less on the technology than on the workforce that must operate, supervise and answer for it. At the same time, the EU Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA), the EU Artificial Intelligence Act, PRA Supervisory Statement SS1/23 and the UK Senior Managers and Certification Regime (SM&CR) turn staff competence and human oversight into regulatory obligations. Existing research covers RegTech, workplace automation and technology acceptance separately, but not their combined effect on workforce management in regulated banking GRC.

The study examines how AI-driven transformation in UK and EU banking GRC is reshaping workforce management decisions on role structure, skill requirements, staffing and employee adaptation, and develops a practical framework for GRC and HR leadership. It follows a sequential exploratory mixed-methods design. Written semi-structured interviews with GRC practitioners and senior professionals produced ten themes. A structured questionnaire, built on constructs adapted from Davis (1989), Glikson and Woolley (2020) and Brougham and Haar (2018), measured how strongly practitioners agree with these themes, reported as means and standard deviations. The themes were then translated into hypotheses that were tested statistically on four independent public datasets: Eurostat enterprise AI-adoption data for 27 EU countries, the annual reports of large UK and EU banks for FY2022 and FY2025, UK financial-services job adverts, and published industry surveys. Paired t-tests, Wilcoxon signed-rank, McNemar, Mann–Whitney U, chi-square and z-tests were applied, and all evidence was triangulated against four research propositions.

The findings show that routine, rules-based GRC work is automated first, while judgement-intensive work is augmented rather than replaced. AI adoption among large EU enterprises roughly doubled between 2021 and 2025, and AI references in bank annual reports rose about tenfold between FY2022 and FY2025, increasing in every bank in the paired sample. References to DORA, the AI Act and SS1/23 and the share of banks naming a dedicated AI governance body also rose significantly. AI-exposed job adverts demand markedly more data, automation and cyber skills. Reskilling disclosure, however, grew far more slowly than AI disclosure and was not linked to AI intensity. Practitioners rated training adequacy lowest among skills items and identified the loss of institutional knowledge, and a weak understanding of AI among accountable senior managers, as the main hidden risks.

The study concludes that AI in GRC recomposes roles rather than removing them, that regulation makes competence and oversight auditable, and that banks' workforce response lags their AI ambition. It proposes a seven-stage workforce transformation framework: diagnose, protect knowledge, redesign roles, build oversight competence, reskill and source, secure trust, and lead change. Knowledge protection precedes any restructuring. The framework gives GRC and HR leaders a sequenced, regulation-aware plan for managing the transition.
